About me

I am a physical oceanographer interested in understanding how ocean circulation shapes water-mass pathways and their variability. I completed my PhD in Oceanography and Global Change at the University of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria (Spain), in December 2025 with Cum Laude distinction. My doctoral research focused on the structure and variability of the Western Boundary Current System of the Weddell Gyre.

My work brings together hydrographic and velocity measurements, satellite observations and ocean reanalyses. I enjoy working across these different sources of information, developing reproducible analyses and exploring the connections between ocean dynamics and biologeochemical processes.

I have co-authored four peer-reviewed research articles, including two as first author. My international experience includes a doctoral research stay at the Alfred Wegener Institute in Germany, a graduate traineeship at the University of the Azores in Portugal, and an undergraduate exchange at the Universidad Autónoma de Baja California in Mexico. I have participated in two oceanographic cruises and coastal sampling campaigns, contributed to national research projects, and presented my work at international conferences. Alongside research, I have co-supervised one Bachelor’s and two Master’s theses, delivered more than 170 hours of university teaching, and actively participated in science outreach.
