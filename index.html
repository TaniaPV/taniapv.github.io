About me

I am a physical oceanographer interested in understanding ocean circulation, water-mass transport, and how physical processes influence biological and biogeochemical variability. I enjoy bringing together different perspectives and methods to address questions linking ocean physics, biogeochemistry and marine ecosystems. Alongside research, I value teaching, mentoring students and making ocean science accessible beyond academia.

CV summary

I hold a PhD in Oceanography and Global Change from the University of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria (2025, cum laude, International Mention). My doctoral research focused on the Western Boundary Current System of the Weddell Gyre, investigating current structure, frontal dynamics, transport variability and their driving mechanisms.


My work combines ship-based hydrographic and velocity measurements, satellite observations, ocean reanalyses and model outputs. I use these complementary sources to investigate circulation and water-mass pathways through transport calculations, model–observation comparisons and Lagrangian particle-tracking experiments. Through multidisciplinary collaborations, including the COUPLING II project, I also explore the links between ocean circulation, connectivity and phytoplankton variability.


My research experience includes a doctoral stay at the Alfred Wegener Institute in Germany, participation in oceanographic cruises and coastal sampling campaigns, peer-reviewed publications and contributions to international conferences. Alongside research, I have experience in university teaching, supervision of Bachelor’s and Master’s theses, and educational innovation. I also contribute to scientific peer review and public engagement through school talks, science events and editorial activities.
